## Design specificity and strengths

StudyBuddy has a coherent green palette, rounded controls and compact information hierarchy. Its visual vocabulary is somewhat interchangeable with other student products; its distinctiveness comes from institution-backed trust, instant booking and equal tutor/tutee roles. Make those product rules clearer at decisions rather than adding decoration.

Three things worth preserving:
- Public landing and login reflowed cleanly on mobile, with clear calls to action.
- The role switch and verification onboarding explain account continuity and document carryover. Mode switching includes announcements and an Undo path.
- Payment summaries place tutor, subject, date, hours and amount together; payout confirmation gives new destinations extra review.

## Five priority issues

### 1. [P1] Tutor reputation can be invented by fallback values

A tutor with zero sessions becomes **124 sessions**, and a zero rating becomes **4.7**, because the profile uses `Number(value) || fallback`. Missing data receives the same defaults. Empty subject lists can also turn into invented subjects.

This directly affects whom a student chooses and conflicts with the product's evidence standard. Preserve zero, distinguish “No ratings yet” from missing data, and remove fabricated profile content.

Evidence: [TutorDetails.vue:369](/C:/FIles/Projects/Apps/Studybuddy/FrontEnd/studybuddy-ui/src/views/TutorDetails.vue:369), initial values at line 317 and response mapping at line 640. Source-verified; not reproduced with a live account.

The public landing also presents unsupported timing/match numbers and says access is open to any campus, despite partner-institution gating. Review those claims with the same standard: [LandingPage.vue:450](/C:/FIles/Projects/Apps/Studybuddy/FrontEnd/studybuddy-ui/src/views/LandingPage.vue:450).

Suggested command: `$impeccable harden`, with `clarify` for public claims.

### 2. [P1] The booking screen gives the wrong cancellation cutoff

Before confirmation, the tutor page says free cancellation ends **24 hours** before the session. The shared frontend configuration and backend use **12 hours**. After confirmation, another message gives the actual deadline.

Students should not have to reconcile two policies when committing their time. Show the authoritative deadline and explain when a booking has no penalty-free cancellation window.

### 3. [P1] Core journeys have keyboard and accessible-name gaps

Login and registration labels are visually present but not associated with their inputs. Browser inspection confirmed the missing associations. In the authenticated sessions source, opening details depends on clicking a table row; the Actions cell is empty.

Associate labels with controls, add appropriate autocomplete, and put a real “View session” link in each row. Review dialog focus and Escape behavior as part of the same journey.

### 4. [P1] “Remove Selected” conceals a recurring schedule deletion

The weekly calendar places dated blocking controls beside “Remove Selected.” That action deletes the recurring availability record immediately. A tutor clearing one Tuesday could remove that teaching time every week.

Label the action “Delete weekly slot,” state its scope, and provide confirmation or Undo. Keep dated blocking clearly separate.

### 5. [P2] Public mobile layouts work better than the operational layout rules

Public pages reflowed successfully, but measured landing action heights were about **34–36px**, below the skill's 44px target. In source, the authenticated shell retains a 250px expanded sidebar and generous main padding without a mobile switch. Sessions uses fixed-height containment and nonwrapping filters; admin detail panels specify 480px and 760px widths.

Increase touch targets, adapt the shell to phone navigation, cap panel widths to the viewport, and keep session identity and its next action visible together. Preserve useful desktop density. The tutor schedule already changes to two columns on phones.

## Cognitive load, emotional journey and personas

Search exposes up to six preference dimensions before results. That information is useful, but optional filters should be clearly secondary. The five session status filters are conventional; fitting them and their actions on a phone matters more than reducing their number. Admin algorithm weights are legitimate expert controls and already have explanatory scaffolding.

The entry experience is calm. Unclear schedule scope introduces uncertainty while tutors publish hours; cash payment introduces another valley at the end of a successful session. “Receipt Image” does not explain what a student should photograph after handing another student cash, what is acceptable, or what happens after submission. That policy needs an owner decision before copy is written.

- **First-time tutee:** misleading reputation and campus-access claims can distort expectations; cash proof needs explanation.
- **Keyboard/screen-reader user:** unnamed auth inputs and pointer-only session rows create barriers.
- **Distracted mobile student:** public pages fit, but small targets and gated layout risks deserve attention.
- **Dual-role student:** preserve the existing role parity, switch announcements and document carryover.

Other observations: failed search requests can end at “No tutors available” after logging only to the console (FindTutors.vue:629); wallet loading can leave default zero/empty content without a visible failure state (TutorWallet.vue:671). Distinguish loading, empty, stale and failed states, and provide Retry. Rename wallet “Performance Track” to a direct earnings label and prioritize available balance. Use neutral styling for “Unrated.”
